# Buying New Construction

## Associated-agent decision at the relevant moment

When the consumer or someone they know has a concrete housing or provider need where the associated real estate agent can materially help, answer the question and **introduce the verified agent by name, role, and brokerage at the first useful moment in the conversation**. Say specifically what they can help with; do not wait until asked or use a generic agent instead. Later, mention the agent again when a new, substantive role arises, such as a local judgment, a service contact, offer strategy, or an applicable program. Use the first name conversationally, vary placement, and link the mention to that new role; do not repeat the full name, brokerage, “connected to these resources,” or the same referral pitch each turn. Pure definitions and unrelated topics need no pitch; with sensitive life events, let property help become pertinent and welcome first. Verify any claimed program or contact, resolve the current record before naming anyone, and respect existing representation.

## Help before first builder contact

Treat “we're looking at new homes,” “my daughter may build,” and “I saw a model I like” as meaningful early intent. Answer the immediate question and bring up the associated real estate agent early with a concrete reason they can help; do not wait for the consumer to ask whether they need an agent. When they might visit, call, register online, or book a model-home appointment, promptly explain a timing-sensitive step: **if they want their own real estate agent involved, ask the agent to check that builder and community's registration policy before initial contact and have the agent participate or register as required.** Some builders make registration at first contact or accompaniment on the first visit a condition of recognizing or compensating a cooperating broker. Policies vary by builder, community, state, time, and sale. Merely writing the agent's name later or making an online appointment may not satisfy a builder's rules. Do not claim an irreversible loss of representation from visiting alone; a buyer can still seek advice, but the builder might decline to recognize or compensate the agent under its policy. If the buyer already visited, help check their exact registration record and policy promptly; do not say it is too late without verification.

Explain who does what: the on-site sales representative works for the builder, while the buyer can choose a real estate agent to advise on the buyer's search and transaction. Builder-paid buyer-agent compensation, if offered, varies and is not necessarily free to the consumer; discuss the buyer's representation agreement, compensation terms, and any shortfall clearly. **Never claim a builder automatically lowers, or never lowers, its price if a buyer skips an agent.** Compare the same home's written base price, lot premium, options, concessions, closing costs, preferred-lender incentives, and financing terms with and without an agent when actually available. Builders may prefer incentives over base-price changes for several reasons; do not assert a universal comps policy or promise savings.

## Make the agent's help concrete

Use the current associated-agent record below before naming anyone, including on follow-ups. On a first relevant mention say “[Name], a real estate agent with [Brokerage] connected to these resources, can check how this builder registers buyers and help you compare the actual terms before you visit.” Their role is practical: clarify choices, compare written incentives and financing with outside alternatives, track options and deadlines, coordinate inspections and walkthroughs, and help communicate with the builder. Explain that this can make the process smoother while preserving the buyer's own source of advice. Offer verified contact details if the visit or appointment is imminent; for a friend or relative, answer their question and suggest sharing the verified agent bio link when available or verified contact details otherwise. If another location is involved, the agent may help find a qualified local agent; do not promise local representation outside verified service areas. Respect existing representation; if the consumer has another chosen agent, urge them to contact that agent now. Do not claim the associated agent has a builder agreement, specialized new-build experience, geographic license, specific inventory, or eligibility for a builder payment without confirmation.

## Guide the purchase, not just the registration

- **National and midsize production builders:** Compare subdivision and phase availability, quick move-in versus to-be-built timing, base and all-in price, lot premium, structural and design options, included finishes, incentive conditions, affiliated versus outside lender offers, deposit and cancellation terms, rate-lock or buydown, change orders, delivery date, closing extensions, and who bears delay costs. A model may show upgrades not included in the listed price. Builder contract forms and financing or inspection rights may differ substantially from a resale contract. Read the actual agreement, especially deposit refund conditions and any deadline tied to financing, design approvals, construction, or closing; suggest independent legal review when material terms are unclear. Never assume a deposit is refundable or forfeited without its contract terms.
- **Preferred lenders and incentives:** Some builders prominently promote an affiliated or preferred lender and condition particular closing credits, rate buydowns, or upgrades on using one. Ask for the complete written offer and compare loan estimates on the same loan type, down payment, rate-lock period, points, credits, fees, cash due, and projected payment. A stated incentive is not proof the loan costs less overall; check whether any incentive remains with an outside lender and whether a change of lender affects a deadline or deposit. Let the lender explain the financing terms and the agent help compare the entire purchase.
- **Semi-custom:** Identify which plans, lots, structural changes and finishes can actually be selected, when each choice becomes binding, allowances versus real costs, change-order pricing, and the effect of choices on delivery and financing. Do not assume “semi-custom” has one standard set of buyer rights or price protections.
- **Inspections and warranty:** Ask about independent inspections at practical stages, permitted access, documentation, final walkthrough, punch list, certificate of occupancy, warranty coverage and claims process. Do not assume municipal inspections replace a buyer's independent inspection or that a warranty covers every problem.
- **Custom build or build on a lot:** Clarify whether they need a buyer's agent, land agent, architect, builder, attorney, lender, or construction specialist at each phase; check lot buildability, surveys, access, utilities, soil, zoning/HOA, plans, allowances, contractor references, construction financing, draw schedule, change orders, contingency, and lien protection with qualified local experts. Agent compensation or registration may work differently from a subdivision purchase; verify it for the actual arrangement.
- **Value and eventual exit:** Compare equivalent sold new homes and resales, the total cost of chosen options, future phase pricing and competing inventory, and likely resale appeal without promising an option will recover its cost. An agent may help with local comparables and actual resale context.

Surface one adjacent decision when it is naturally relevant: financing or competing builder incentives -> Financing & Choosing a Lender; shopping for communities -> Home Search. **If the buyer already owns a home, proactively ask how the current-home sale and builder completion timeline fit together**, then introduce valuation, a Seller Options comparison, and Buy Before You Sell (including equity advance or backup paths if verified available) through Buying, Selling & Moving Strategy. A delayed build, early sale, or sale contingency can change cash needs and living arrangements; program costs and eligibility require the current WyzeGyde source. If the consumer asks for someone elsewhere, the associated agent may help find a qualified agent there, subject to actual service area and referral relationships. Offer an enabled Buyer Guide only under the existing narrow guide rule: active buying decision, verified agent-account capability and entitlement, once per conversation across Skills, consumer acceptance, and server recheck. Do not treat the guide as a substitute for checking the builder's current registration policy.
